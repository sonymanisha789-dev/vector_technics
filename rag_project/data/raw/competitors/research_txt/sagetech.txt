## 0. CORRECTION TO THE PREMISE

The task described Sagetech Avionics as "based in Sandpoint/Sun Valley Idaho." **That is incorrect.** Every primary and secondary source checked places the company in the Columbia River Gorge, Washington State:

- Company's own site footer and contact block: **317 W Steuben Street, Bingen, WA 98605** (https://sagetech.com/about-us/who-we-are/, https://sagetech.com/about-us/careers/)
- USAspending.gov recipient record (UEI T4UNAUKXMZM3): `317 W STEUBEN ST, BINGEN, WA 98605-9006`, congressional district WA-04
- HigherGov awardee profile: Bingen, WA (https://www.highergov.com/awardee/sagetech-avionics-inc-10280151/)
- Older press releases datelined **White Salmon, WA** (the adjacent town, across from Bingen); some databases (CB Insights, ZoomInfo) still list a White Salmon address at 1000 E Jewett Blvd
- Careers page explicitly: *"We're located in Bingen, WA, in the Columbia River Gorge, which is about an hour east of Portland."*

No evidence of any Idaho office, relocation, or satellite facility was found. The likely source of confusion is **Quest Aircraft Company / Kodiak Aircraft**, an unrelated aircraft manufacturer in Sandpoint, Idaho (acquired by Daher in 2019). Treat the Idaho attribution as a false premise; the rest of this report uses the verified Washington facts.

### 1.2 Origin story and corporate history

The lineage runs through a predecessor, **Sagetech Corporation** (separate UEI N3GCB5FTDN79, DUNS 030467026), an engineering services firm in the Gorge that supported Boeing's Insitu — doing engine dynamometer testing, electronics, microelectronics, software and firmware work.

Key milestones:

- **~2004–2006** — Sagetech Corporation wins early federal R&D contracts including two DARPA contracts (HR0011-04-C-0119, ~$396.9K, Sept 2004; HR0011-06-C-0008, ~$397.0K, Oct 2005) and an Army AMCOM contract (W31P4Q-05-C-R174, ~$399.1K, April 2005). Source: USAspending.gov.
- **~2009** — Boeing/Insitu needed a transponder for small drones that were invisible to radar; existing transponders were too big and heavy. Sagetech Corporation built what it calls **the world's first micro transponder** — initially a circuit card for the ScanEagle, later productized into a business-card-sized unit. (https://www.auvsi.org/news/oem-leader-how-sagetech-avionics-builds-layers-of-aviation-safety-for-dual-use-bvlos-capabilities/, https://sagetech.com/about-us/who-we-are/)
- **2012–2019** — XP-series transponders ship broadly; NASA, NOAA, Navy and Army purchase orders appear in USAspending under Sagetech Corporation (e.g., NND12AE16P Mode C transponders 2012; NND14AB72P "Mode transponder with mini ADS-B" 2014; N68335-14-C-0407 Navy SBIR Phase I Topic N142-102, ~$79.8K, Sept 2014; DOC RA133R15SU0276 NOAA AirCore sampling system transponders 2015; 80NSSC17P1164 NASA "two MX-MP-03 Sagetech ADS-B transponders" 2017; N66001-18-P-0182 "engineering services in support of the ScanEagle UAV transponder system" 2018).
- **November 2019** — **Columbia River Partners** (a Seattle/Portland/San Francisco private investment firm specializing in "corporate divestitures, recapitalizations, carve-outs and other complex situations in the lower middle market") executes a carve-out of the avionics division / acquisition of the transponder IP from Sagetech Corporation, forming **Sagetech Avionics, Inc.** Tom Furey, who had been COO of Sagetech Corporation for five years, leads the acquisition "with a few like-minded individuals" and becomes CEO. (https://crgta.org/spotlight/sagetech-avionics-inc, https://www.columbiariverpartners.com/)
- **2020–2021** — Company adds military IFF functionality and develops the MX12B Mode 5 platform; receives DoD AIMS certification for the Mode 5 micro IFF transponder; wins AFWERX contract; wins 2021 AUVSI XCELLENCE Award for Technology for the MX12B; demonstrates Mode 5 Level 2B Out at Bold Quest 2021.
- **June 2021** — $12M investment from Due West Partners.
- **February 2023** — FAA certifies the **MXS**, described as the first FAA-certified small transponder with integrated ADS-B In.
- **April 2026** — $248.5M, 10-year Army IDIQ for the AN/APX-128.

## 5. PRODUCTS

Sagetech organizes its catalog into three go-to-market verticals (Government–Defense, Government–Civil, Commercial) over three product families: **transponders**, **ACAS/DAA solutions**, and **systems integration services**. There is also an interrogator line (MXR) and a newer OEM/military line (ACX-3000M, AN/APX-128).

### 5.1 MX Series — current-generation transponders

The MX Series is marketed as "transponders and interrogators [delivering] ADS-B In and Out, IFF Mode 5, detect and avoid, omni-directional surveillance, and other advanced functionality." **MXS and MX12B share a common hardware platform**, which is the line's key commercial argument: OEMs can swap between military and civil configurations **without additional integration, software changes, or aircraft changes.**

#### MX12B — Mode 5 IFF Micro Transponder (defense)
*(https://sagetech.com/transponders/mx12b/)*

Positioning: **"the world's first AIMS-certified micro Mode 5 IFF transponder"**; the smallest full-function Mode 5 IFF transponder available; **the only micro Mode 5 IFF transponder fully compliant with the DoD AIMS Mk XIIB specification**; the **first Mode 5 Level 2B Out transponder certified**.

Consolidates into one enclosure: ADS-B In/Out, pressure altitude encoder, antenna diversity, Ethernet — eliminating discrete components at integration.

Claimed advantage vs. traditional Mode 5 transponders: **93% smaller, 6x lighter, 5x less expensive.**

Won the **2021 AUVSI XCELLENCE Award for Technology**. Demonstrated **Mode 5 Level 2B Out at Bold Quest 2021**.

It is the **cornerstone of Sagetech's DAA architecture** — its integrated ADS-B In is the critical cooperative-traffic sensor feeding the ACAS X logic.

#### MXR — Micro Interrogator
Referenced in partnership coverage rather than on a dedicated product page at time of research. The MXR is an **active-surveillance micro interrogator** used alongside the transponders. In the **Hover, Inc.** partnership, the MXR + MXS combined with Hover's embedded computing lets a UAV **actively interrogate nearby aircraft, validate ADS-B In tracks, and resolve threats** — described as compliant with **ACAS-Xu and ACAS-Xr** and aimed at UAVs flying **above 400 ft BVLOS in the National Airspace System**. Sagetech has publicly argued that "transponders and interrogators will serve as core technology enablers for future DAA systems required on autonomous aircraft as Urban Air Mobility integrates into manned airspace."

#### ACX-3000M
Named in the APX-128 press release as one of the two existing transponder lines the AN/APX-128 extends (alongside MX12B). A military transponder line; no dedicated public product page found.

#### AN/APX-128 — Advanced IFF Transponder (newest)
The subject of the April 2026 Army award. Fully **MOSA (Modular Open Systems Architecture) compliant**, includes **ADS-B In** aligned with anticipated **ALERT Act** requirements, and is **upgradeable to ACAS Xr** collision avoidance. Extends the MX12B and ACX-3000M lines. Low SWaP-C replacement for legacy Army IFF hardware. Rapidly developed under a separate SBIR contract with partner **R Cubed Engineering**. Fields with government-provided **KIV-79** crypto appliqué (per the USAspending delivery-order descriptions). Target platforms: **Army helicopters, other DoW assets, and Foreign Military Sales.**

### 5.2 XP Series — legacy transponders

All XP-family units share **FCC ID: YT5XP-TR**. All are "designed to FAA TSO standards" and "can be seen by TCAS."

### 6.2 What is known about Sagetech's IP position

**The 2019 transaction was explicitly an IP acquisition.** The Gorge Technology Alliance profile states Sagetech Avionics "was founded in 2019 through the acquisition of **intellectual property** from Sagetech Corporation." This means:
- Any patents filed before ~2019 would be assigned to **Sagetech Corporation** (UEI N3GCB5FTDN79), not Sagetech Avionics, Inc.
- Ownership would have transferred via the carve-out, which would show as an **assignment record** in the USPTO Patent Assignment Database — the right place to look when a search tool is available.
- Searching only "Sagetech Avionics" as assignee will under-count the portfolio.

**Search strings to use when a patent database is reachable:**
- Assignee: `"Sagetech Corporation"`, `"Sagetech Avionics, Inc."`, `"Sagetech Avionics Inc"`, `"Sagetech Holdings"`
- Inventor: `Furey`, `Hamilton` (Matthew), `Day` (David) — note that Hamilton and Day both came from ACSS/L3Harris and Honeywell, so their earlier patents would be assigned to those firms, not Sagetech
- CPC classes: **G01S 13/78** (secondary radar / IFF transponder systems), **G01S 13/76**, **G08G 5/04** (aircraft collision avoidance), **G08G 5/0021**, **H04B 7/185**

**Likely subject matter, inferred from product claims** (these are technology areas, NOT confirmed patents):
- Miniaturization of a full-function Mode A/C/S transponder into a ~100 g / 3.5″×1.8″×0.7″ package — the "world's first micro transponder" claim
- Integration of Mode 5 IFF (Levels 1 & 2), Mode S, ADS-B In and ADS-B Out, pressure altitude encoder, and antenna diversity into a single 190 g enclosure at full AIMS 17-1000 transmit power "with no deviations"
- The **"SafeMission+"** software/technology branding, applied to both the MXS transponder and the ACAS DAA computer — a named, likely-trademarked technology block
- Combining a micro **interrogator** (MXR) with ADS-B In track validation for cooperative DAA, per the Hover, Inc. architecture
- MOSA-compliant, field-upgradeable IFF architecture (APX-128) supporting later insertion of ACAS Xr

**Countervailing evidence that the portfolio may be modest:** Sagetech's public positioning leans overwhelmingly on **certifications** (AIMS Mk XIIB, AIMS Mk XA, FAA TSO, FCC, ANATEL) and **flight-hour track record** (1.5M hours) rather than on patents. Its marketing materials, leadership bios, and press releases contain **zero patent claims** — notable, because the company does loudly claim "world's first" repeatedly. The one place patents are mentioned on the whole site is advisor **Douglas Kirkpatrick's** bio ("60+ patents"), and those are his personal patents from Fusion Lighting/SAIC/DARPA work, not Sagetech assets. In a certification-gated market like IFF/ADS-B, the **AIMS and TSO certificates are the real moat** — they take years and millions to obtain and are far harder for a competitor to replicate than a patent is to design around.

**Trademarks** are a more likely IP register to yield hits: "Sagetech," "SafeMission+," "MX12B," "MXS" are all candidate marks worth checking in USPTO TESS.

**Recommendation for follow-up:** run USPTO Patent Public Search (ppubs.uspto.gov) with `AN/"Sagetech"` and `AANM/"Sagetech"`, plus the USPTO Patent Assignment Search for assignments **from** Sagetech Corporation **to** Sagetech Avionics dated late 2019. Also check whether **R Cubed Engineering** holds co-invented APX-128 IP from the SBIR — SBIR data rights (not patents) are often the operative IP mechanism in Phase III sole-source awards like W15P7T-26-D-A001.

## 8. SYNTHESIS AND ANALYTICAL NOTES

**What Sagetech actually is:** a ~68-person, PE-backed, HUBZone-certified small manufacturer in rural Washington that occupies a narrow but strategically valuable niche — the smallest certified cooperative-surveillance radios in aviation. Its defensibility is regulatory, not patent-based: AIMS Mk XIIB, AIMS Mk XA, FAA TSO, and FCC certifications on a sub-200-gram package that competitors have not matched.

**The inflection point is April 2026.** Before the APX-128 award, Sagetech's federal prime business was under $2M/year and lumpy — a $150K AFWERX award, some $30–45K hardware buys, and two FAA research contracts. The Army IDIQ changes the company's category: a $248.5M ceiling over ten years, with **$8.56M already obligated in four delivery orders within three months of award**. The sitemap shows the company hiring a Quality Technician, Supply Chain Manager, and Inventory & Logistics Specialist — the signature of a design shop becoming a production house.

**Strategic thesis (the "layered" story):** Sagetech's three published blog/op-ed pieces in 2026 all argue the same line — cooperative broadcast (transponder/ADS-B) is the reliable layer, non-cooperative sensing is the unreliable layer, and the gap is low-altitude close-coordination flight. The commercial expression of that thesis is: sell the certified transponder (MXS/MX12B/APX-128), make it the cooperative sensor for the ACAS X DAA computer, and plug in third-party non-cooperative sensors (MatrixSpace, Iris Automation) rather than building them. That is a deliberately asset-light, integration-centric architecture.

**Dual-use hedge:** the MXS/MX12B shared hardware platform lets an OEM flip between civil and military configurations with no aircraft change. That single design decision is why Sagetech can simultaneously serve Joby's Part 135 eVTOL certification path and the U.S. Army's helicopter IFF refresh from one production line.

**Key risks visible in the public record:**
1. **Customer concentration** — FY2026 federal revenue is essentially one program (APX-128) with one customer (Army PEO/PM at Aberdeen, contracting office W15P7T).
2. **Regulatory dependency** — ACAS Xu/Xr/sXu revenue is gated on FAA MOPS maturation (DO-365B, DO-386) and on BVLOS rulemaking; the ALERT Act, cited as justifying APX-128's ADS-B In, was still "currently in Congress" as of April 2026.
3. **Supply chain / crypto** — APX-128 fielding depends on government-furnished **KIV-79** crypto appliqués (explicit in three of four delivery orders), a dependency Sagetech does not control.
4. **Scale mismatch** — executing a $248.5M ten-year production program with ~69 people is a real operational stretch; the hiring pattern suggests management knows it.
5. **Site data hygiene** — the XPG product page appears to display MX12B specs, and the "Active Contracts and Past Performance" page is a live placeholder marked `[need content]` in its source. Minor, but worth knowing if you are ingesting sagetech.com into a RAG corpus: **the site contains at least one internally inconsistent spec block.**
