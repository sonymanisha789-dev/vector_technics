# RESEARCH REPORT: Starlogic Intelligent Technology (星逻智能 / SKYSYS)

## 0. METHODOLOGY AND A CRITICAL NAMING NOTE — READ FIRST

"Starlogic Intelligent Technology" is the English name used by Western startup databases (principally Tracxn) for the Chinese company **星逻智能科技（苏州）有限公司** — *Xingluo Zhineng Keji (Suzhou) Co., Ltd.* The name is a direct calque: 星 (xing, "star") + 逻 (luo, "logic").

The company does **not** market itself as "Starlogic" in English. Its own English-language brand is **SKYSYS** (skysys.cn). If you search only for "Starlogic Intelligent Technology" you will find essentially one thin database entry; the substantive record lives under 星逻智能 and SKYSYS.

**Explicit caveat on English-language availability:** very little reliable English-language material exists on this company. The single English aggregator profile (Tracxn) contains several claims that are flatly contradicted by primary Chinese sources and by the company's own website — see Section 8. The bulk of this report is sourced from Chinese-language tech press, the company website, and Chinese venture databases. Where figures conflict, I state both and flag the conflict rather than reconciling them silently.

**Sources I attempted and could not access** (so you know what is missing rather than assuming it does not exist): Google Patents (HTTP 503), Espacenet (HTTP 403), FreePatentsOnline (connection reset), USPTO legacy search (DNS failure), Qichacha company registry (blocked). As a result, Section 6 gives a patent *activity summary* with aggregate counts from secondary sources but **no individual patent numbers or titles** — I could not verify any, and I am not going to invent them.

### 1.2 Founding story

The company was founded in 2017 by three people who met in industry, two of them at General Electric. The origin story, told in detail by The Paper ([thepaper.cn](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_21336616)), is that co-founders 钱茂冬 (Qian Maodong) and 王海滨 (Wang Haibin) were on an overseas business trip and observed a contradiction: mining haul trucks had already been automated, yet drone operations still depended entirely on human pilots, making them expensive and non-scalable. They identified 2016 as the inflection point at which drones began migrating from consumer/entertainment products into industrial production settings — chemicals, power, security, environmental inspection — and judged the enabling layer to be a market worth "hundreds of billions" (千亿级) of RMB.

They left GE and began operating as a project team within three months. Dr. 肖素枝 (Xiao Suzhi) joined roughly three months after that, supplying the software and algorithms capability the two hardware engineers lacked.

### 1.3 Strategic positioning — the defining decision

The single most important strategic fact about this company is what it refuses to build. Co-founder Qian Maodong has stated the position bluntly ([thepaper.cn](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_21336616)):

> "如果我们做无人机，相当于站在了所有无人机的对立面" — *"If we made drones, we would effectively be standing in opposition to every drone maker."*

SKYSYS therefore builds **everything around the aircraft but never the aircraft**: docking stations/hangars, automatic charging and battery-swap systems, onboard AI compute modules, autonomous-flight middleware, cloud dispatch, and vertical AI inspection software. Its hardware is deliberately compatible with mainstream commercial airframes — DJI M30, M350 RTK, M3D/M3TD and others.

The stated ambition is to become infrastructure, not a product vendor: Qian has described the goal as building "全国最大的无人机低空调度网络" — the country's largest low-altitude drone dispatch network — and has analogised the hangar network to petrol stations for ground vehicles.

A key technical differentiator claimed is **纯网联技术** ("pure network-connected technology"): the drones connect over cellular networks rather than depending on a ground radio link to a local controller. This allows point-to-point missions — take off from Hangar A, land at Hangar B — rather than the out-and-back-only pattern that radio-tethered competitors are limited to. For transmission-line inspection, this enables "leapfrog" operation between hangars spaced along a power corridor ([skysys.cn](https://www.skysys.cn/DockingStation/), [thepaper.cn](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_21336616)).

### 1.4 Mission and current framing

The company's self-description has evolved. Early framing (2018-2021) was generic drone automation and smart city. Current framing, post-2023, is explicitly new-energy focused:

> "以AI驱动无人机与机器人完成能源检测与运维闭环，提升发电效率" — *driving drones and robots with AI to close the loop from energy inspection through to O&M, raising generation efficiency.* — Wang Haibin ([stcn.com](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/1224287.html))

Wang has articulated the underlying market thesis: compared with building new power stations, **raising the output efficiency of the existing installed base is the cheaper and faster path** — which is the entire commercial premise of the inspection-plus-cleaning-robot product line.

The company describes itself as "国内头部的低空智能化系统供应商" (a leading domestic supplier of low-altitude intelligent systems) and claims to be "国内首家出口海外的无人机自动化供应商" — the first Chinese drone-automation supplier to export overseas ([36kr pitchhub](https://pitchhub.36kr.com/project/1678486488167426)).

## 2. FINANCIALS

**Bottom line: SKYSYS is a private, venture-backed company. No revenue figures, gross margins, profitability, or valuations have been publicly disclosed in any source I could access, Chinese or English. There are no financial filings because it is not listed on any exchange and no IPO process has been announced.**

What *is* on the public record:

**Registered capital:** RMB 4,166,880 ([news.qq.com](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20240606A07RGP00)). Note that in Chinese corporate practice registered capital is a legal formality and bears no relationship to capital actually raised — this company has raised orders of magnitude more than its registered capital.

**Ownership:** Equity-penetration analysis reported by Tencent News shows Wang Haibin as the ultimate controller with an ultimate beneficial stake of **22.3501%** ([news.qq.com](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20240606A07RGP00)). No other shareholder percentages have been disclosed. A ~22% founder stake after eight rounds is consistent with a heavily institutionally-financed cap table.

**Cumulative capital raised (estimate):** Aggregating the disclosed and banded round sizes in Section 3 produces roughly **RMB 300 million or more** (very approximately USD 40–45 million at prevailing rates). This is an estimate, not a disclosed figure, because five of the eight rounds were announced only in bands ("数千万" = tens of millions; "超亿元" = over one hundred million).

**Valuation:** Not disclosed for any round. Tracxn's valuation fields were masked/redacted. However, two circumstantial data points bracket it upward:
- Inclusion in the **《2025年度苏州市"独角兽"培育企业名单》** (Suzhou 2025 unicorn-*cultivation* enterprise list), announced January 2026 ([skysys.cn/News](https://www.skysys.cn/News/)). Note carefully: "培育" means *cultivation* — this is a pipeline list of companies deemed capable of *becoming* unicorns, not a certification that the company is valued above USD 1 billion. Do not report this as unicorn status.
- Two consecutive nine-figure RMB rounds (B and B+) within twelve months, with strategic and state-linked investors participating.

**Revenue signals (indirect only):** The Paper reported in 2023 that the 2021 refocus onto three verticals produced "台阶式增长" — step-change growth — in both revenue and headcount, but gave no figures ([thepaper.cn](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_21336616)). The business-model shift disclosed at the B round is material for revenue quality: the wind product 御风 is moving to a "产品即服务与服务分润" model — product-as-a-service with revenue sharing — via franchise partnerships with wind O&M providers, which implies a transition from one-off hardware sales toward recurring service revenue ([stcn.com](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/1224287.html)).

**Tracxn's "$1.49M total raised" figure is wrong** and should not be used. It omits the 2024 B round and 2025 B+ round entirely, each of which alone exceeded RMB 100 million (~USD 14M). Tracxn's own news section even cites the June 2024 "over 100 million yuan" report while the aggregate field contradicts it.

### 3.2 Reading the cap table

Three observations worth flagging:

**(a) Extraordinary insider persistence.** 远瞻资本 (Lighthouse) participated in the Pre-A, A, A+, A++ and B rounds — five consecutive rounds. 遨问创投 (CM Venture) led the angel and then followed in Pre-A, A, A+ and B. 常春藤 (Ivy) joined at A and was still adding at B. Angel-round investors doubling down at a nine-figure B round six years later is a meaningful positive signal.

**(b) Progressive shift to state and strategic capital.** The early rounds are pure financial VC. From the A+ onward, state-linked vehicles appear (Zhongguancun Qihang, Oriza/Suzhou Industrial Park), and by B and B+ the leads are provincial state fund-of-funds (Guangdong Yueke), a solar-industry strategic (the Arctech / 阿特斯 Yangzhou fund — Arctech is a major PV tracker manufacturer, so this is a customer-adjacent strategic), and a municipal SOE (Suzhou Port & Shipping). This pattern is typical of Chinese hard-tech companies approaching pre-IPO positioning.

**(c) The Arctech tie is strategically significant.** 临港前沿阿特斯扬州基金 links SKYSYS to the PV tracker/module supply chain — precisely the installed base its inspection and cleaning robots service.

(All quotes from [stcn.com](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/1224287.html) and [chinaerospace.com](https://www.chinaerospace.com/article/24066).)

### 3.4 Use of proceeds

- **B round (2024):** primarily mass production and technical iteration of the **Lantern PV cleaning robot**, specifically the goal of a "flying" cleaning robot capable of working on 45°-tilt arrays, completing the loop "从低空智能检测向低空智能运维" (from low-altitude intelligent inspection to low-altitude intelligent O&M). Secondarily, moving the 御风 wind-inspection product into accelerated service-model rollout.
- **B+ round (2025):** use of proceeds not disclosed in the sources I could reach.

## 5. PRODUCTS

SKYSYS organises its portfolio into four layers: **hangars/docking stations** (hardware), **onboard compute** (edge AI), **flight and dispatch software** (OS layer), and **vertical AI inspection applications** — plus a newer fifth line, **embodied robots**. All descriptions and specifications below are as published by the company at [skysys.cn](http://www.skysys.cn/).

### 5.1 Drone hangars / docking stations — the UltraHive family

A vehicle-mounted/portable hangar operable by a single person, supporting automatic takeoff, monitoring and return, enabling routine cloud-edge coordinated inspection away from fixed infrastructure.

**(6) DJI DOCK 2 integration**

SKYSYS resells/integrates DJI's own Dock 2 as a lightweight, easily deployed unattended platform paired with the dedicated Matrice 3D / 3TD aircraft; 34 kg excluding the aircraft. Notable as evidence of the non-competitive posture toward DJI.

**Deployment concept:** hangars are intended for gridded (网格化) deployment to create a "无人机五分钟出勤圈" — a five-minute drone response circle — with a stated target of two minutes to launch and three minutes to arrive on scene for emergency response. For transmission and substation inspection, hangars are spaced along the line so drones leapfrog between them.

### 5.2 Onboard compute — SkyCab® 天斗 ([specs](https://www.skysys.cn/SkyCab/))

A lightweight airborne AI computing terminal — effectively the edge-inference brain that turns a stock commercial drone into an autonomous inspection platform.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Compute module | NVIDIA **Xavier NX**; **up to 21 TOPS**; upgrade path to Orin NX stated |
| Deep learning accelerators | 2× NVIDIA engines |
| Vision accelerator | 7-channel VLIW vision processor |
| Memory | 8 GB / 16 GB, 128-bit LPDDR4x, 59.7 GB/s bandwidth |
| Storage | 128 GB eMMC, TF card expandable; industrial-grade SSD options in 8G/16G specs |
| Weight | ~145 g including antenna and basic bracket |
| Dimensions | 84 × 56.5 × 30 mm |
| Ingress protection | **IP55** — all-weather outdoor operation |
| Drone interface | DJI **OSDK / PSDK** standard hardware interfaces; direct coaxial-cable connection to M30, M350 and similar |

Supported third-party payloads cited: high-altitude loudspeakers, dual-light (visible + thermal) cameras, parachutes.

### 5.3 Flight and dispatch software — SkyScout® 祺云 ([page](https://www.skysys.cn/SkyScout/))

The remote drone operating system / dispatch layer. Web client plus PAD (tablet) client. Supports defining and dispatching inspection tasks; central operators remotely control both drones and hangars and monitor operations; on-site personnel can manually take over an aircraft via the PAD client for real-time response. Handles route planning, task scheduling, remote control, and real-time cloud upload of data.

### 5.4 Vertical AI inspection software — the four-energy suite

The company markets four vertical applications covering what it calls 光电风气 — solar, grid, wind, and gas/emergency ([page](https://www.skysys.cn/AISoftware/)):

**(1) 星逻驭光 — Skysys PV Inspector** (flagship). Flies predefined waypoint routes covering an entire solar site, delivering a closed O&M loop: fully automated inspection → data upload → diagnostic report → defect rectification → re-inspection verification. Combines drone thermal/visual data with **inverter diagnostics** to produce whole-lifecycle diagnosis of a PV plant.

**(2) 星逻巡电 — Skysys Electricity Inspector.** Grid and transmission-line inspection: automated route import, tower route editing and generation, autonomous flight execution, AI defect analysis, one-click defect report generation. Marketed for conductor and ground-wire (导/地线) inspection.

**(3) 星逻御风 — Skysys Wind Turbine Inspector.** Wind turbine blade/tower inspection. Productised for two years as of 2024. Supports **three operating modes**: unattended (无人值守), turbine-stopped service (停机服务), and turbine-running service (不停机服务). Delivered at multiple power-generation group sites. From 2024 being pushed through a franchise/partnership model with wind O&M service providers on a product-as-a-service and revenue-share basis.

**(4) 星逻万象 — Skysys Universal Inspector.** Emergency response and security platform. Frees industrial drones from single-platform lock-in and combines aerial and ground perspectives into a dual-view picture, for multi-scenario low-altitude urban coverage.

### 5.5 Embodied robots — the Skysys Lantern line

Categorised on the site as **低空具身机器人** ("low-altitude embodied robots") — the 2023-onward expansion from *detection* into *maintenance*, and the primary use of B-round proceeds.

**Skysys Lantern PV panel cleaning robot.** Currently marketed in two models: **Lantern 小蓝 (Small Blue)** and **Lantern 大蓝 (Big Blue)**. Earlier reporting described a blue "dry-clean" variant for dust and mud in arid regions and a red variant using cleaning fluid for stubborn residue such as bird droppings ([kjc.shlingang.com](https://kjc.shlingang.com/kjc/xwzxs/yqxw/202405/t20240531_31574.shtml)).

Distinguishing characteristics:

- **Drone-deployed and drone-retrieved.** The robot is airlifted onto and off panels by drone, enabling automated placement on complex terrain — rooftops, hillsides, tidal flats — that ground crews cannot easily reach. The company calls this the world's first deep integration of a drone with a cleaning robot.
- **Negative-pressure adhesion** conforming to panel tilt angle.
- **Traverses inclines up to 45°** — the explicit engineering target of the B-round funding.
- **Autonomous operation:** depth algorithms drive self-positioning, path planning and cleaning, with real-time status telemetry.
- **Verification loop:** a follow-up drone inspection pass confirms cleaning quality — closing the loop with 驭光.
- **Claimed outcome: 8%–30% increase in power generation** ([stcn.com](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/1224287.html)). Note this is a company claim, not independently verified, and the range is wide enough to suggest heavy site-dependence (desert sites with heavy soiling would sit at the top of the range).
- Lightweight design for one-person transport.

R&D began in 2021; the product went through "两年多次迭代" (two years of repeated iteration) before launch in 2023.

## 8. DATA QUALITY ASSESSMENT AND CONTRADICTIONS

For a RAG stress-test corpus, the contradictions are as informative as the facts. Documented conflicts:

**(1) Headquarters: Shanghai vs Suzhou.** Tracxn says Shanghai. The company's own site and its legal registration say **Suzhou Industrial Park**, with Shanghai (Lingang and Pudong) as R&D/office sites. The likely explanation is that early operations centred on the Shanghai Lingang project (2018–2020) before the entity was established/relocated in Suzhou; the legal entity name itself contains "（苏州）". **Suzhou is correct for HQ.**

**(2) Employee count: 3 vs ~160.** Tracxn's "3 employees as of 30 June 2026" is not credible against ten offices, a nine-figure 2025 round, a national Little Giant designation, and continuous 2026 activity. **~160 with 70% R&D is the credible figure.**

**(3) Total funding: $1.49M vs RMB 300M+.** Tracxn's aggregate omits both nine-figure rounds. **Tracxn is wrong.**

**(4) Founders: one vs three.** Tracxn lists only "Victoria Xiao." Chinese sources consistently name **three co-founders**: Wang Haibin (CEO/chairman), Qian Maodong, and Dr. Xiao Suzhi. "Victoria Xiao" ≈ Xiao Suzhi.

**(5) Wang Haibin's experience: "16 years" vs "nearly 20 years."** Different sources, likely different vintages of the same boilerplate.

**(6) PV deployment scale: 427 stations / 8.3 GW vs ~400 / 6 GW vs 540+ / 10 GW.** Partly genuine growth over time, partly inconsistent source vintages. The 540+ station / 10 GW figure is the most recent.

**(7) Tracxn's competitor set** (uAvionix, Sagetech, ARK Electronics, Zerodrag, Defendron) is a category mismatch — those are avionics/transponder/ADS-B companies, not drone-hangar or energy-inspection companies. SKYSYS's real competitive set would be drone-in-a-box vendors (DJI Dock itself, 复亚智能 Fuya, 中科云图 ZhongkeYuntu, 道通 Autel), PV O&M robotics firms, and energy inspection software vendors. Tracxn's "6th of 68 competitors" ranking should be disregarded.

**(8) Products described by Tracxn** — "a proprietary drone with payload up to 1 kg and 25 minutes endurance," "a docking system in the form factor of a drawer," "GPS and VPS dual positioning" — describe an early (circa 2018–2019) product generation and directly contradict the company's explicit, repeatedly stated policy of never building drones. This is stale data from the company's earliest seed-stage description.

**Recommended source hierarchy for this company:** (1) skysys.cn product pages for specifications; (2) Chinese business press (stcn.com, 36Kr, Tencent News) for funding and metrics; (3) thepaper.cn for founder and history detail; (4) innohere / 36Kr pitchhub for round-by-round financing tables; (5) Tracxn — **low reliability, use only for the English name mapping.**

## 9. SUMMARY

Starlogic Intelligent Technology / 星逻智能 / SKYSYS is an eight-year-old, Suzhou-headquartered Chinese company of roughly 160 people that sells the infrastructure layer for autonomous industrial drone operations — hangars, chargers, onboard AI compute, dispatch software — plus vertical AI inspection applications, and since 2023 a line of drone-deployed PV cleaning robots. It deliberately does not manufacture aircraft, positioning itself as a neutral enabler to DJI and other airframe makers rather than a competitor.

It has raised eight rounds since 2018, with the last two (B in June 2024, B+ in June 2025) each exceeding RMB 100 million, backed increasingly by provincial state funds, a PV-industry strategic (Arctech), and a municipal SOE. Founder-CEO Wang Haibin retains ~22.4%. No revenue, valuation or filings are public.

Its commercial centre of gravity has shifted decisively from smart-city/security (2018–2021) to new-energy asset O&M (2022–present), anchored by the 驭光 PV inspection platform (540+ stations, 10+ GW covered) and the award-winning Lantern cleaning robot (claimed 8–30% generation uplift). It holds 150+ patents including pending applications and 120+ software copyrights — though no individual patent numbers could be verified for this report — and co-drafted IEEE 1937.1 with Huawei and DJI. It is on Suzhou's unicorn-cultivation list, holds national Little Giant status, and in 2026 signed an MoU with France's JPA Group while its Lantern robot won a Red Dot award.

**English-language coverage of this company is genuinely sparse and, where it exists, substantially inaccurate.** Anyone building an information product on this company must work from Chinese sources.
