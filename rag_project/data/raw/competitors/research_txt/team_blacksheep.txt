# TEAM BLACKSHEEP (TBS) — RESEARCH REPORT
Compiled 2026-09-24.

## 0. CORRECTION TO BRIEF

"Founder Michael 'Fishpepper'/Raphael Pirker" conflates two unrelated people. Raphael Pirker ("Trappy") is the actual founder/CEO. "fishpepper" = Simon Schulz, an independent German open-source hardware developer (tinyFISH FC, tinyPEPPER ESC, OpenSky firmware) with no relationship to TBS. Also: Tracxn's "Blacksheep" profile is an unrelated Chennai media production company (180 employees) — a false-positive trap for retrieval.

## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

Origin: early-2000s Swiss FPV scene. Raphael Pirker, a University of Zurich student, began FPV flying ~2003 in the Swiss mountains with a foam-wing aircraft and hacked baby-monitor electronics, eventually setting an 89km long-range FPV record. Name/team origin dated 2007 by the company itself. Early collaborators: Christoph Dunkel and Remo Masina.

Founding date is contested across sources: 2007 (brand origin), 2009 (fpv24.com), 2010 (Crunchbase), 2013 (BlackSheep Avionics HK incorporation). Best reading: informal team from ~2007, commercial entity from ~2010, current HK manufacturing entity from 2013.

Current corporate structure — three entities, three jurisdictions:
- **BlackSheep IP Holdings Pte Ltd** (Singapore) — HQ, R&D, support; Pirker is CEO here.
- **BlackSheep Avionics Co. Ltd** (Hong Kong/Kowloon) — factory/logistics, also filed as TBS Avionics Limited on FCC grants.
- **Conceptos Pirker GmbH** (Austria, St. Anton am Arlberg) — EU office/returns.

Size: 14 employees at the HK entity per Bounce Watch (likely HK-only, not group-wide). Self-reported: "10+ Years of Experience," "2M+ Systems installed," 1M+ flight hours across the Tracer ecosystem. Engineers described as spanning USA, Europe, Asia.

Mission/positioning: storefront tagline "Serious Toys." About page: "redefine the FPV hobby one flight at a time," in an industry "dominated by imitation." Singapore holdco frames itself more corporately: "a defining R&D company in advanced radio control, telemetry, and IoT hardware" — signaling ambitions beyond hobby FPV. 10+ years of free, continuous firmware updates as a durable commercial promise.

### The FAA case — defining public episode
Pirker became famous flying a fixed-wing FPV aircraft over Lower Manhattan/Statue of Liberty (posted to YouTube), and TBS filmed similar stunts in the Taj Mahal, Dubai, Miami, Rio, London, Hong Kong. Labeled "aerial anarchists" by Australian media. Legal chain:
- 2011: FAA proposes $10,000 penalty against Pirker for a commercial shoot over University of Virginia, calling the flight "careless or reckless."
- Oct 2013: motion to dismiss filed, arguing FAA had no enforceable rule covering model aircraft.
- 6 Mar 2014: NTSB Administrative Law Judge dismisses the penalty — widely treated as the foundational US drone-regulation precedent (Huerta v. Pirker). Gave FPV pilots ~8 months of relatively unrestricted flying before rules tightened again.
- TBS publicly stated it reinvests considerable revenue into R&D for safer, more reliable civilian drones.

## 6. PATENTS

No patents attributable to Team BlackSheep, TBS Avionics, BlackSheep Avionics, or BlackSheep IP Holdings were found; none naming Raphael Pirker as inventor. Patent-title searches surfaced only unrelated documents (Parrot, other RF/antenna patents). Google Patents and Justia both blocked automated retrieval (503/403) — the negative result is partially search-limited, not conclusive.

TBS's actual regulatory paper trail is equipment authorization, not patents: FCC ID QOS-TX2W (TBS Crossfire, applicant TBS Avionics Limited, registered 2016-11-08), FCC ID QOS-TANGO2, EU Declarations of Conformity for TBS Avionics Co Ltd products.

Assessment: TBS's moat is protocol lock-in (CRSF), firmware/cloud ecosystem, brand/community, and RF type-approval engineering — not patents. Company has historically operated in an open/community-adjacent way (open-source Source One frame, FreedomTX as an OpenTX fork, free perpetual firmware). "IP Holdings" in the corporate name most likely refers to trademarks/copyrighted firmware, not patents.

**Competitive context — ExpressLRS:** the single most consequential development in TBS's market is external. ExpressLRS (open-source, GPLv3, LoRa/FSK on Semtech transceivers) began 2018 at 900MHz/200Hz, added 2.4GHz plus cheap commercial hardware in 2020 driving mass adoption, now supports up to 1000Hz packet rates and 100km+ range. FPV pilots have gravitated to ExpressLRS over Crossfire/FrSky due to lower cost and better latency; it's near de facto standard status in the FPV community by the mid-2020s. Since 2022, ExpressLRS has also become dominant in Ukraine-conflict FPV strike drones (EW resistance, open-source adaptability). TBS pioneered long-range-link/telemetry/OTA-update/cloud-sync features and has since watched an open-source project commoditize the category — the Tracer's speed/latency positioning, CRSF's ubiquity (ELRS itself speaks CRSF), and the Lucid high-voltage push all read as responses to that squeeze.

**Historical timeline:** 2011 FAA penalty proposed; Oct 2013 motion to dismiss; 6 Mar 2014 NTSB dismisses penalty; Jul 2017 FLARM collision-avoidance released; Oct 2017 Oscar Liang reviews PowerCube Elite; 21 Jun 2018 TBS-associated pilot races Marc Márquez's F1 car with an FPV drone; Mar 2020 "Quarantine Masters" Velocidrone racing series (pandemic-era); Feb 2022 TBS Agent Lite released; 14 Feb 2023 Oscar Liang's consolidated Crossfire/Tracer setup guide.

## 8. ASSESSMENT

TBS is a founder-controlled, bootstrapped, three-jurisdiction (Singapore IP holdco / Hong Kong manufacturing / Austria EU office) FPV hardware company built on a YouTube-famous origin and a landmark FAA legal case (Huerta v. Pirker). No public financials, no funding history, no patents (moat is protocol lock-in via CRSF, firmware/cloud ecosystem, and brand). Currently consolidating hardware onto Nano-form-factor links and the Mambo/Tracer radio platform, pushing into higher-voltage/industrial airframe support (Lucid 16S ESC, MAD motors), and facing real competitive pressure from the open-source ExpressLRS project that has commoditized much of what TBS pioneered.
