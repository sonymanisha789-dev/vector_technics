# uAvionix Corporation — Research Report

## Disambiguation (Read This First)

There are **at least two distinct companies with similar names** that are easy to confuse. This report is about **uAvionix Corporation** (styled "µAvionix" in some branding), the aviation/UAS avionics company headquartered in Bigfork, Montana. It should **not** be confused with:

- **UVIONIX / Uvionix Aerospace Corporation** — a completely different company, founded in 2014 in Rancho Cordova, California by Boris Iskrev and Yordan Iskrev (with Stanislav Darmonski later involved). UVIONIX builds autonomous *indoor warehouse* drones (its flagship aircraft is called "U-VeE," a compact coaxial drone) for inventory management, paired with "Uvionix.Cloud" software. It has raised roughly $3.54M across 2 rounds from 4 investors — an entirely separate market (warehouse robotics/logistics) from uAvionix's aviation-safety/avionics focus. (Sources: [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/uvionix), [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/uvionix-aerospace-corporation/__weRRSuTLZ7_73pwPQRW7JeTXwxqoLaU2tb7r7-tApbQ), [uvionix.com](https://www.uvionix.com/about))
- Search-index noise: CB Insights lists a separate profile under "Univonix," which appears to simply be a data-entry variant/typo of "Uvionix" rather than a third distinct company — no independent entity by that exact name was found.

Everything below concerns **uAvionix Corporation**, the Bigfork, Montana-based ADS-B/transponder/avionics company.

**Notable positioning:** uAvionix bills itself as providing an "unparalleled engineering and management team" spanning avionics, surveillance, airport services, UAS aircraft development, RF engineering, and semiconductor design backgrounds. ## 5. Products

uAvionix's catalog spans **three broad market segments**: (A) General Aviation / crewed-aircraft avionics, (B) Uncrewed Aircraft Systems (UAS/drone) avionics and safety systems, and (C) Airport/ATC ground infrastructure. Below is the fullest catalog assembled from company materials and industry coverage.

### A. General Aviation (Crewed Aircraft) Products

- **skyBeacon** — uAvionix's original certified wing-tip-mounted ADS-B Out solution; integrates ADS-B radio, GPS, and antennas into a replacement position/strobe light housing, cutting installation time to minutes versus traditional panel-mount ADS-B installs. This product line was the subject of the Garmin patent lawsuit (see Patents section).
- **tailBeacon / tailBeaconX** — Tail-mounted counterpart to skyBeacon; tailBeaconX is a certified ADS-B transponder solution leveraging tail-mounted antenna placement for optimal signal performance to both ground-based and space-based (satellite) surveillance infrastructure. Covered by U.S. Patent 11,011,830 ("Wireless Communication System within an External Enclosure for Attachment to a Vehicle," issued June 8, 2021).
- **echoUAT** — A 978 MHz Universal Access Transceiver (UAT)-based ADS-B solution, also implicated in the Garmin litigation alongside skyBeacon.
- **ping978EC** — A newly launched (March 2026) 978 MHz UAT ADS-B transceiver, part of a broader Portable Electronic Conspicuity (PEC) push, launched alongside a new "Pilot Awareness Website" educating pilots on PEC devices and aligning with the FAA's proposed Part 108 BVLOS rule.
- **SkyEcho / SkyEcho 2** — A portable ADS-B In/Out transceiver conforming to the UK CAA's Electronic Conspicuity standard; broadcasts an aircraft's position, altitude, course, and speed to nearby aircraft. Popular in the UK/European general aviation and gliding markets (distributed via uavionix.store, the company's European storefront).
- **AV-30-C (certified) / AV-30-E (experimental)** — A panel-mounted, 3⅛-inch multifunction electronic flight instrument replacing legacy vacuum-driven gauges. Functions as an Attitude Indicator, Directional Gyro, Slip/Skid indicator, Angle-of-Attack (AoA) indicator, or multifunction display (MFD), with 4 base modes and 14+ functions. Mounts from behind the panel to preserve the classic "round dial" look without panel cutting.
  - Technical specs: attitude rate limit ±250°/sec; attitude operational range 360° roll / 180° pitch; AV-30-E airspeed range 40–300 knots; altitude range −1,000 to +25,000 ft; AoA reliable up to ±8G, operational range −10° to +30°, valid at 25–300 knots.
  - **AV-HSI** — Expansion module adding IFR-approved lateral/vertical (glideslope) deviation display for enroute and approach navigation; integrates with Trio and TruTrak/Aerocruze autopilots; connects up to four AV-30-C units and provides an ARINC 429 interface to digital autopilots. Received FAA certification (PMA) around June 2025.
  - **AV-APA** — Autopilot-integration module, certified alongside AV-HSI, described by the company as a "major step forward in IFR flight capability for general aviation."
  - **AV-Link** — Wi-Fi bridge module (~$349) enabling live ADS-B traffic display, wireless firmware updates, and (as of February 2026, via software v3.2.1) ForeFlight route-building/transfer and autopilot coupling on certified AV-30-C installs.
  - **SkyPlay** — A jointly announced uAvionix/ForeFlight product unveiled around AirVenture Oshkosh 2026; further technical details were not available in this research pass.
- **NanoHead** — Transponder control head/interface product (per company patent/product listings).
- **truFYX / truFYX EXT** — GPS position-source products supporting ADS-B position integrity requirements.

- **ping200XR** — Combines the TSO-certified ping200X transponder with an internal TSO-certified aviation-grade GPS receiver in one unit; achieved FAA TSO certification around August 2024.
- **pingRX / pingRX Pro** — A Detect-and-Avoid (DAA) ADS-B receiver for professional UAS, detecting cooperative aircraft on both 978 MHz (UAT) and 1090 MHz (Mode S/ES) frequencies. Marketed as providing "nearly 100x greater range" than radar, camera, or FMCW-based detect-and-avoid alternatives, giving operators more reaction time. Compatible with ArduPilot, PixHawk, and Auterion Skynode autopilot/flight-controller platforms for rapid integration. Described by the company as the evolution of the skyBeacon/tailBeacon lineage applied to the UAS market.
- **pingStation** — Ground-based ADS-B receiver station product (per company catalog listings).
- **pingUSB** — USB-based ADS-B receiver, likely aimed at developers/integrators and ground control station use.
- **pingEFB** — ADS-B receiver product oriented toward Electronic Flight Bag (EFB) integration.
- **Legacy ping2020 / ping2020i / ping1090 / ping1090i** — Earlier-generation ADS-B transceiver/receiver products in the "ping" family (predecessors to the current ping200X/pingRX lines).
- **Casia X** — Airborne, onboard optical Detect-and-Avoid (DAA) system (originating from the Iris Automation acquisition/technology lineage) that senses non-cooperative aircraft (those not broadcasting ADS-B) using patented computer-vision and AI, fused with integrated ADS-B for cooperative-aircraft coverage. Automatically executes collision-avoidance maneuvers via turnkey autopilot integration; reports detected intruders to ground station/pilot-in-command in real time; low Size-Weight-and-Power (SWaP) footprint for small UAS; detects/classifies small single-engine aircraft, single-rotor helicopters, birds, and multirotors; designed/assembled in the USA; FCC Part 15 and Industry Canada compliant.

## 6. Patents

uAvionix has actively built and defended a patent portfolio, and patent strength has been directly relevant to a major legal dispute in its history.

- **U.S. Patent 10,156,627** — "Aircraft Navigation Light ADS-B Radio" (the "Power Transcoder" patent). Application accepted by USPTO announced December 4, 2018; issued December 18, 2018. Covers technology synchronizing squawk code and Mode C altitude data between an aircraft's secondary-surveillance-radar (SSR) transponder replies and its ADS-B transmissions — notably by detecting electrical noise induced on the aircraft's power wiring during transponder transmission, rather than capturing the data over the air. This design underlies the skyBeacon/tailBeacon/echoUAT product family and was central to defending against Garmin's infringement claims (see below).
- **U.S. Patent 11,011,830** — "Wireless Communication System within an External Enclosure for Attachment to a Vehicle." Issued June 8, 2021. Covers leveraging wing/tail mounting locations on an aircraft to optimize transponder performance for both ground-based and satellite-based surveillance infrastructure; underlies the **tailBeaconX** product.
- **U.S. Patent 10,991,260** — "Intelligent Non-Disruptive ADS-B Integration for Unmanned Aircraft Systems (UAS)." Granted April 27, 2021. Covers a method allowing UAS to gain ADS-B safety benefits ("inert and alert" capability) while minimizing radio-spectrum utilization — relevant to the UAS/drone ADS-B receiver product line (pingRX, ping200X family).
- **U.S. Patent 10,890,657** — "ADS-B transponder system and method." Relates to vehicle-control systems incorporating 978 MHz UAT transponders for aircraft that must also carry 1090 MHz Mode A/C transponders, addressing FAA ADS-B dual-frequency compliance.
- **Patented computer-vision/AI detection technology** underlying the **Casia X/Casia G** optical detect-and-avoid systems (inherited via the Iris Automation acquisition) — used to sense and classify non-cooperative aircraft (those without ADS-B) via camera and machine-learning models; specific patent numbers for this technology were not identified in available public sources during this research pass and would require direct USPTO assignee-name searches under "Iris Automation" for full enumeration.

**The Garmin Litigation (IP Moat Context):** In June 2018, Garmin International sued uAvionix in Montana federal court, alleging that uAvionix's skyBeacon and echoUAT products infringed Garmin's **U.S. Patent 8,102,301**, specifically regarding methods of obtaining transponder Mode 3/A code and altitude data for ADS-B broadcast. uAvionix denied infringement, asserting it used a different, patent-pending method (the Power Transcoder approach later patented as US 10,156,627). The case was closely watched industry-wide as a proxy for how market leader Garmin (dominant in GA panel/remote-mount avionics) would respond to lower-cost new entrants. The dispute settled on confidential terms on **October 17, 2019** — no injunction was issued, no damages were publicly disclosed, and uAvionix retained the right to continue manufacturing and selling skyBeacon, tailBeacon, and echoUAT. uAvionix's own December 2018 patent grant (during the litigation) was publicly framed by the company as evidence the USPTO recognized "distinct and innovative differences" between its solution and Garmin's.

**IP strategy / moat assessment:** uAvionix's patent strategy centers on protecting the specific *engineering methods* that let it build low-cost, minutes-to-install ADS-B hardware (the transcoder/power-line-noise-detection approach, and wing/tail-mount RF engineering) rather than broad platform patents. This is a defensible but narrow moat — it protects specific implementations against direct copying (as demonstrated by successfully surviving the Garmin suit) but does not obviously block competitors from building ADS-B hardware via different engineering approaches. The 2023 Iris Automation acquisition materially broadened the IP moat by adding computer-vision/AI-based non-cooperative-aircraft detection — a different technical approach (optical vs. RF/transponder-based) that diversifies uAvionix's defensible technology base and reduces reliance on any single patent family. Given the company's now-diversified position across (a) certified GA ADS-B/EFIS hardware, (b) UAS-specific ADS-B/DAA hardware, (c) optical DAA (via Iris), and (d) government airport-surface-surveillance contracts (FlightLine/VTU-20), its moat is increasingly a combination of patented hardware techniques, FAA certification/TSO approvals (a significant regulatory barrier to entry in itself), and incumbency in government procurement (e.g., the sole-source-feeling FAA SAI and VMAT contracts), rather than patents alone.

## 7. Latest News (2024–2026)

- **August 2024 (approx.):** uAvionix achieved **FAA TSO certification for the ping200XR**, its combined Mode S transponder and internal TSO-certified GPS receiver for UAS.
- **October 2024:** uAvionix's **FlightLine Airborne Position Reference Tool** was first installed at **Glacier Park International Airport** (Kalispell, Montana), an early proof point for its airport-surface-safety expansion.
- **June 2024:** uAvionix received its **first approvals under the FAA Surface Awareness Initiative (SAI)** program, aimed at improving controller situational awareness and reducing runway-incursion risk.
- **March 2025:** uAvionix announced that its **Casia G** ground-based optical detect-and-avoid system gained **nighttime aircraft-detection capability**, enabling round-the-clock (24/7) BVLOS drone operations — a significant operational unlock for commercial drone operators previously restricted to daylight BVLOS flights.
- **June 2025:** uAvionix announced **FAA certification of its AV-HSI and AV-APA** modules for the AV-30-C, described as "a major step forward in IFR flight capability for general aviation," enabling full instrument-approach navigation guidance and digital-autopilot integration on legacy GA aircraft equipped with the AV-30-C.
- **November 2025:** The **FAA awarded uAvionix a contract to expand FlightLine SAI deployment to 55 additional air-traffic-control towers** (on top of 14 already operational), with a 12-month installation/full-operational-status target, plus deployment of **VTU-20 VMAT units on FAA Technical Operations vehicles** so ground vehicles appear on the same displays as aircraft. This rollout is explicitly tied to the FAA's push to equip all its towers with surface-awareness technology within two years and aligns with the agency's "Brand New Air Traffic Control System" (BNATCS) modernization vision.
- **January 2026:** *Avionics News* (Aircraft Electronics Association publication) ran a feature headlined "FAA Approves uAvionix," consistent with continued regulatory momentum (specific certification not fully detailed in available excerpts).
- **February 2026:** uAvionix released **AV-30 software v3.2.1**, bringing **ForeFlight integration and autopilot coupling** to the certified AV-30-C (previously available only on the experimental AV-30-E), letting pilots build/modify flight routes in ForeFlight and wirelessly transfer them via the optional **AV-Link** Wi-Fi bridge module ($349).
- **March 2026:** uAvionix launched the **ping978EC**, a new 978 MHz UAT ADS-B transceiver, alongside a new **"Pilot Awareness Website"** educating pilots on Portable Electronic Conspicuity (PEC) devices — positioned explicitly around the FAA's proposed **Part 108** rulemaking for BVLOS drone operations.
- **May 2026:** The **FAA procured uAvionix VTU-20 VMAT units for a $16.5 million contract** (including installation and ongoing service) to equip roughly **2,000 FAA Technical Operations vehicles** nationwide with ADS-B, closing a long-standing surface-visibility gap between ground vehicles and aircraft/controllers.
- **August 2026:** uAvionix and **Airwise Solutions** announced a strategic partnership to support **Skyway Range**, integrating uAvionix's **FlightLine** surveillance data into Airwise's "Nexus" common operating picture — pitched as letting BVLOS operators avoid deploying their own local sensors in favor of consuming "FAA-grade" airspace-traffic data as a service. Around the same time (AirVenture Oshkosh 2026), uAvionix and **ForeFlight** unveiled a jointly branded product called **"SkyPlay,"** though granular product details were not available in this research pass.

**Overall 2024–2026 narrative:** uAvionix has continued to diversify beyond its original consumer/GA and drone-hobbyist ADS-B hardware roots into being a significant **FAA infrastructure contractor** (airport surface surveillance, vehicle ADS-B) while simultaneously deepening its **general aviation avionics** ecosystem (AV-30 family plus ForeFlight integrations) and its **BVLOS-enabling detect-and-avoid** technology (Casia nighttime capability, Part 108-aligned PEC push). This reflects the strategic logic of the 2022 DC Capital Partners buyout and 2023 Iris Automation acquisition: leveraging private-equity backing and broadened optical/AI DAA capability to win larger, higher-value government and infrastructure contracts rather than relying solely on smaller-ticket avionics hardware sales.
