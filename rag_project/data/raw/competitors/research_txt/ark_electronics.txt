# ARK Electronics, LLC — Research Report

---

## 0. DISAMBIGUATION (read first)

This report covers **ARK Electronics, LLC** of Murray / Salt Lake City, Utah, USA — the open-source-focused designer and manufacturer of PX4/ArduPilot drone flight controllers, GNSS/RTK modules, and robotics electronics, trading as **arkelectron.com**, founded 2018 by Alex Klimaj.

It is NOT about:

- **Ark Electronics, Inc.** (Irvine, California) — an electronics manufacturing services (EMS) / contract manufacturer. Unrelated ownership, unrelated products, unrelated market.
- **Suzhou Good-Ark Electronics Co., Ltd.** (Shenzhen Stock Exchange: 002079) — a Chinese discrete-semiconductor maker (diodes, rectifiers, power devices). Unrelated.
- **Ark Invest / ARK ETFs**, **Ark Robotics**, or any similarly named entity.

Distinguishing identifiers for the subject company: domain `arkelectron.com` (note: no "ics" in the domain), GitHub org `github.com/ARK-Electronics`, LinkedIn `linkedin.com/company/ark-electronics-llc`, Utah entity number 10997430-0160, X/Twitter handle `@ARK_Electr0nics`.

### 1.4 Mission and positioning

The homepage tagline is **"USA Made Drone and Robotics Electronics,"** with a hero line of **"Mission-Critical Drone Electronics."** The stated value proposition is "NDAA-compliant, Blue UAS listed flight controllers, GPS modules, and sensors" for "defense, commercial, and research applications" (https://arkelectron.com/).

The About page frames the founding purpose as designing and building American-made electronics for drones and robotics that are "trusted for professional, commercial, and defense applications," emphasizing domestic supply chain, quality control, American manufacturing jobs, and continuous innovation (https://arkelectron.com/about/).

Trust badges displayed on the homepage: Made in USA, NDAA Compliant, Blue Framework Listed, Dronecode Foundation Member, ArduPilot Partner, 47G Member.

Strategically, ARK occupies a specific niche: **open-standard, open-source, US-manufactured autopilot hardware for the defense-adjacent drone market.** Its products are built to published open hardware standards (Pixhawk FMUv6X, Pixhawk Autopilot Bus / PAB) rather than proprietary form factors, which makes them drop-in alternatives to Chinese-manufactured boards (Holybro, CUAV) for buyers who need NDAA Section 848 compliance and Blue UAS Framework listing.

### 1.5 Relationship to PX4 and the Dronecode Foundation

This relationship is central to the company's identity and is unusually deep for a company of its size.

**Membership.** ARK Electronics joined the **Dronecode Foundation as a Silver Member**, announced in a post dated **September 22, 2023** (https://dronecode.org/welcome-ark-electronics-as-a-silver-member/), with a corresponding company announcement "ARK Electronics Joins Dronecode" on **October 1, 2023** (https://arkelectron.com/news/page/3/). Dronecode's current member roster confirms Silver tier, alongside peers including CUAV, Holybro, ModalAI, Freefly Systems, NXP, SIYI, DroneBlocks, Neros, TII, and Firestorm. Platinum tier is held by Auterion, DeltaQuad, and UVify (https://dronecode.org/our-members/).

- ARK added **four new DroneCAN peripherals** to the PX4 ecosystem in 2025, extending PX4 beyond flight controllers into standardized CAN-bus sensor modules.
- Non-code contribution is also noted: Discord support, GitHub issue triage, weekly PX4 developer call participation.

(https://dronecode.org/the-2025-year-in-review/)

**Upstream hardware support.** ARK boards are documented in the official PX4 user guide as **"manufacturer supported"** targets — see the ARKV6X page (https://docs.px4.io/main/en/flight_controller/ark_v6x) and the ARK FPV page (https://docs.px4.io/main/en/flight_controller/ark_fpv). The only way to bound revenue is from observable operating signals, which should be treated as inference rather than fact:

- **58 SKUs** currently listed on the webstore, with unit prices from **$20** (adapters) to **$3,587.15** (top-configuration Jetson PAB V3 NDAA bundle). Average selling price for the core flight-controller and GNSS lines sits roughly in the **$150–$1,200** band.

### 3.2 Capital strategy read

The pattern that emerges is **growth financed by revenue and by partnerships rather than by equity**:

- Facility expansion apparently funded organically.
- Distribution and capability gaps filled through **partnerships** (SiMa.ai for AI compute, Mobilicom for cybersecurity, Doodle Labs / Silvus / Microhard / DTC for radio integration) rather than through acquisition or heavy R&D spend.
- A **European expansion** effort staffed by hire rather than by subsidiary capitalization — Tracxn logs the appointment of **Thierry Basse to lead European expansion in June 2023**.

Investment-thesis framing found in secondary analysis: full founder control and zero dilution make ARK an attractive target for a strategic acquirer or defense-focused growth-equity buyer, while the absence of disclosed funding and public revenue is a risk factor given that hardware manufacturing is capital intensive.

## 5. PRODUCTS

ARK's catalog comprises **58 SKUs** across roughly eleven categories, plus airframes documented but not listed in the webstore, plus **ARK-OS** software. Sources: the full webstore pagination (https://arkelectron.com/shop/, /page/2/, /page/3/) and the complete documentation sitemap (https://docs.arkelectron.com/sitemap.md).

The architectural through-line: nearly everything is built to two open standards — **Pixhawk FMUv6X** (the flight-controller electrical/firmware spec) and the **Pixhawk Autopilot Bus (PAB)** (the connector/form-factor spec that lets a flight-controller module plug into any compliant carrier board). This makes ARK's flight controllers and carrier boards independently mixable with third-party hardware, and vice versa. Peripherals standardize on **DroneCAN** over CAN bus.

### 5.1 Flight Controllers

**ARKV6X Flight Controller — $400.00** (flagship)
The company's core product, launched **January 25, 2023**. Built to the FMUv6X and PAB open standards, made in the USA, NDAA compliant, Blue UAS Framework listed (added February 14, 2025). PX4 lists it as a manufacturer-supported target.

Full specs per PX4 documentation (https://docs.px4.io/main/en/flight_controller/ark_v6x):
- MCU: **STM32H743IIK6**, 480 MHz, 2 MB flash, 1 MB RAM
- IMUs: **triple synced** — two Invensense **ICM-42688-P** + one Invensense **IIM-42652** industrial IMU. The triple-synced arrangement enables data averaging, voting, and filtering (i.e. fault tolerance against a single bad sensor).
- Barometer: **Bosch BMP390**
- Magnetometer: **Bosch BMM150**
- Storage: **FRAM** + MicroSD slot
- **1W integrated heater** to hold sensors at temperature in extreme cold (a meaningful accuracy feature — IMU bias drifts hard with temperature)
- Power: 5V input, 500 mA total (300 mA system + 200 mA heater)
- Physical: **5.0 g**, 3.6 × 2.9 × 0.5 cm
- 8 UARTs mapped to GPS, TELEM1–3, RC/PX4IO, debug console, secondary GPS. Firmware note: the ARKV6X bootloader enables only **UART7 (TELEM1)**, so serial firmware upload must use that port.
- LED status indicators

Variants and bundles:
- **ARKV6X Flight Controller Extended Range — $450.00** (extended temperature/environmental range part selection)
- **ARKV6X Bundle — $750.00**
- **ARKV6X Extended Range Bundle — $800.00**
- **ARKV6X-RT** — referenced in PX4 docs as a related board

**ARKV6S Flight Controller — $340.00**
Launched **June 2, 2026**. Explicitly a "cost-effective, single-IMU variant of the ARKV6X." STM32H743 @ 480 MHz, one industrial-grade IMU, 1W heater, FRAM + MicroSD, PAB form factor, USA-built, NDAA compliant. Ships with PX4 preloaded; ArduPilot supported. (https://arkelectron.com/arkv6s-flight-controller-launch/ ; https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2026/06/ark-electronics-unveils-arkv6s-ndaa-compliant-flight-controller-for-uav-robotics-applications/)

The V6S is the strategically interesting SKU: it trades the triple-IMU redundancy for a ~15% lower price than the ARK FPV-class cost tier while retaining the same architecture, aimed at high-volume/attritable platforms where per-unit cost dominates over redundancy.

**ARK FPV Flight Controller — $194.99**
Derived from the ARKV6X but repackaged into the **30.5 mm standard FPV mounting pattern**. Specs (https://docs.px4.io/main/en/flight_controller/ark_fpv):
- MCU: STM32H743IIK6, 480 MHz, 2 MB flash, 1 MB RAM
- IMU: Invensense **IIM-42653** industrial IMU
- Barometer: **Bosch BMP390**
- Magnetometer: **ST IIS2MDC**
- Power: **3–12S battery input**, with **regulated 12V 2A output** for video transmitters and payloads (the distinguishing feature — FPV builds normally need a separate VTX regulator)
- Ships with PX4; optional ArduPilot flashing; **Betaflight support added February 2025** (https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2025/02/ark-fpv-flight-controller-now-supports-betaflight/)

The triple-firmware support (PX4 / ArduPilot / Betaflight) on one board is unusual and is a deliberate wedge into the FPV/racing and attritable-drone market that Betaflight dominates.

**ARK Pi6X — $665.00–$785.00** and **ARK Pi6X Flow — $915.00–$1,035.00**
Integrated flight controller plus Raspberry Pi Compute Module companion computer on one board. The "Flow" variant adds integrated optical flow. Documentation covers block diagram, pinout, CM4 Lite/eMMC flashing guides, Wi-Fi/SSH setup, ARK services, QGroundControl connection, and flight-controller firmware updating (https://docs.arkelectron.com/sitemap.md).

### 5.2 Carrier Boards (PAB)

**ARK Pixhawk Autopilot Bus Carrier — $450.00**
The reference PAB carrier: accepts any PAB-compliant flight-controller module (ARKV6X, ARKV6S, or third-party). Documented with pinout, PX4 instructions, 3D models and case files.

**ARK Jetson PAB Carrier — $800.00**
Combines an NVIDIA **Jetson Orin** module with a PAB flight-controller socket on a single board — i.e. autopilot plus AI companion computer, no inter-board cabling. Runs **ARK-OS**. Documented with full hardware reference: pinout, Mini DisplayPort, block diagram, 3D models and case files, GPIO control, camera overlays, USB port power cycling, flashing guide, micro-USB console.

**ARK Jetson PAB V3 — $800.00** (launched **May 26, 2026**)
Described as "our most advanced Pixhawk Autopilot Bus (PAB) carrier board yet." Key additions over prior generations:
- Integrated **10/100 Ethernet switch** providing native, low-latency Ethernet between the Jetson and the flight controller (replacing serial/UART as the high-bandwidth link — this matters for ROS2/DDS workloads)
- External Ethernet ports
- "Swap-optimized form factor"
- Jetson **Orin NX / Orin Nano** compatible; works with any PAB-compliant FC
- Fully NDAA compliant, USA manufactured
(https://arkelectron.com/ark-jetson-pab-v3-launch/)

**ARK VOXL2 RTK PAB Carrier — $1,150.00**
Carrier integrating ModalAI's VOXL2 compute platform with RTK GNSS and a PAB flight-controller socket. Documented with pinout, connectors and cables, 3D models, autopilot connections.

### 5.3 Embedded / Companion Computers

**ARK Just A Jetson — $680.00**
A standalone NVIDIA Jetson carrier without the integrated flight-controller socket — for builders who already have an autopilot. Extensive documentation: connect/get-online setup, ARK-OS usage, flight-controller firmware updating, pinout, ports and serial, **ICM-42688P IMU guide**, **INA238 power monitor guide**, block diagram, 3D model, GPIO control, camera overlays, USB power cycling, flashing guide, USB-C console.

**Jetson module options.** As of **September 15, 2026**, ARK Jetson bundles offer both **Orin Nano and Orin NX** in multiple memory sizes, including **Orin NX 8GB** (https://arkelectron.com/news/).

### 5.4 GPS / GNSS (the broadest product line — 11+ modules)

ARK fields one of the widest GNSS ranges of any autopilot vendor, spanning three silicon suppliers (u-blox, STMicroelectronics, Septentrio) and both simple UART modules and full DroneCAN RTK units.

**Entry / UART modules:**
- **ARK SAM GPS — $75.00** — u-blox **SAM-M10Q**, 4 concurrent GNSS constellations, jamming/spoofing detection, ST **IIS2MDC** industrial magnetometer, Pixhawk-standard UART/I2C 6-pin JST GH, **36 × 36 × 8 mm, 11 g, 15 mA average**, USA-made, NDAA compliant. **Added to the Blue UAS Framework September 9, 2026** (https://arkelectron.com/ark-sam-gps-added-to-the-blue-uas-framework/). PX4/ArduPilot/Betaflight instructions documented.
- **ARK SAM GPS MINI — $75.00** — compact variant.
- **ARK DAN GPS — $145.00** — announced September 4, 2025. PX4/ArduPilot/Betaflight support.
- **ARK GPS — $225.00** — the original module.
- **ARK TESEO GPS — $215.00–$268.00** — built on the STMicroelectronics **TESEO LIV4F** receiver. A notable non-u-blox option, useful for supply-chain diversification.

**RTK modules:**

## 6. PATENTS

**Summary: no granted patents or published applications assigned to ARK Electronics, LLC, or naming Alex Klimaj as inventor, were located in this research pass. The evidence strongly suggests the company's IP strategy is deliberately not patent-based.**

### 6.1 Search limitations (stated honestly)

Direct patent-database queries were attempted and blocked:
- **Google Patents** (`patents.google.com`, assignee and free-text queries, plus the XHR API endpoint) — returned HTTP 503 on every attempt.
- **Justia Patents** (`patents.justia.com`) — returned HTTP 403.
- **Espacenet** (`worldwide.espacenet.com`) — returned HTTP 403.
- **FreePatentsOnline** — connection reset.
- **USPTO Patent Public Search / PatentsView API** — requires POST or an API key, unavailable via the fetch tooling here.
- Web-search budget for this session was exhausted before a patent-specific search could be issued.

**Therefore "no patents found" here means "none surfaced across company materials, press releases, trade coverage, and investor-database profiles," not "a negative result from an exhaustive patent-database query."** A definitive answer requires a direct USPTO Patent Public Search or PatentsView API query on assignee "ARK Electronics" (Utah) and inventor "Klimaj, Alex."

### 6.2 Affirmative evidence of a non-patent IP posture

Several independent signals point the same direction:

1. **Products are built to open standards ARK does not own.** FMUv6X and the Pixhawk Autopilot Bus are Dronecode/Pixhawk-project open hardware specifications. A company whose differentiation rests on *conforming to* someone else's published spec cannot patent the spec, and gains little from patenting around it.
2. **ARK publishes its own designs as open source.** The webstore describes the ARK RTK GPS as "open source," and `ark-hardware` is a public repository. Open-publication is antithetical to a patent-first strategy (and, once published, starts the clock on any filing window).
3. **Firmware is derived from GPL/permissive upstream projects.** ARK32 derives from AM32; flight controllers run PX4 (BSD-3) and ArduPilot (GPLv3). ARK contributes changes back upstream rather than holding them proprietary.
4. **The Tracxn profile — which does report IP activity when it exists — records no patent data.**
5. **No press release, product page, or datasheet uses "patented," "patent pending," or cites a patent number.** For a hardware company, patent marking is both legally advantageous and standard marketing practice; its total absence is informative.

### 6.3 What ARK's actual defensible moat appears to be

In lieu of patents, the company's competitive protection appears to rest on:

- **Regulatory moat:** NDAA compliance plus **13-15+ DIU Blue UAS Framework listings**, reportedly the most of any component vendor on that list. Framework listing is expensive and slow to obtain and functions as a de facto barrier to entry for the US defense channel — arguably a stronger moat than a patent in this market.
- **Domestic manufacturing:** US design, manufacture, and test, which competitors offshoring to China structurally cannot replicate.
- **Upstream code position:** being the #2 contributor to PX4 means ARK's hardware is supported first and best in the firmware everyone else also uses.
- **Trademark:** the "ARK" and "ARKV6X" marks are presumably protected, though no USPTO trademark registration was verified in this pass (a TSDR lookup would confirm).

### 6.4 Recommended follow-up

- USPTO Patent Public Search: assignee `ARK Electronics` AND state `UT`; inventor `Klimaj`.
- USPTO TSDR / TESS trademark search for `ARK ELECTRONICS`, `ARKV6X`, `ARK-OS`, `ARK32`.
- Google Patents at a later time (the 503s appeared to be transient rate limiting rather than a permanent block).

## 8. ANALYTICAL SUMMARY

**What ARK Electronics is.** A roughly ten-person, founder-controlled, entirely bootstrapped Utah LLC that has, in eight years and with zero outside capital, become the **#2 code contributor to PX4** and the vendor with **the most products on the DIU Blue UAS Framework**. It sells ~58 SKUs spanning autopilots, GNSS/RTK, IMUs, optical flow, rangefinders, ESCs, power, LTE/radio carriers, and Jetson/Raspberry-Pi compute — all designed and built in the USA to open Pixhawk standards.

**The strategy, stated plainly.** Do not invent a proprietary platform; instead implement the open standard better than anyone, manufacture it domestically, get it onto the DIU list, and become the default component supplier for every US drone OEM that needs to be NDAA compliant. Contribute heavily upstream so the firmware everyone uses supports your hardware first. Fill capability gaps with partnerships (SiMa.ai for AI silicon, Mobilicom for cyber, Anduril-Lattice interop for C2) rather than with capital.

**Strengths.** Regulatory moat via Blue UAS listings that competitors cannot quickly replicate. Genuine technical credibility in the PX4 community. Zero dilution and full founder control. Breadth of catalog unusual for the headcount. Multi-firmware (PX4/ArduPilot/Betaflight) and multi-silicon (u-blox/ST/Septentrio; NVIDIA/SiMa.ai/Raspberry Pi) hedging.

**Risks and open questions.** Hardware manufacturing is capital intensive and ARK has no disclosed funding buffer. Key-person concentration in Alex Klimaj is severe. No patent portfolio means no licensing revenue and no defensive IP if a larger player copies the (openly published) designs — the moat is regulatory and reputational, not legal. Revenue is entirely unverified. And as an unfunded, founder-controlled asset sitting on a scarce regulatory position in a hot defense market, ARK is an obvious acquisition target for a defense prime or a growth-equity buyer.

**Gaps in this report requiring follow-up.** (a) Definitive USPTO patent and trademark queries — blocked here by database 403/503 responses. (b) Federal contract award history via USASpending/SAM.gov — requires POST/API access. (c) Current exact Blue UAS Framework product count — the trajectory is 11 (May 2025) → 13 (Aug 2025) → 15+ (Sept 2026), but the live DIU list should be checked. (d) The ARK RIG5 / RIG7 airframes, documented but not sold publicly. (e) Full text of the facility-expansion coverage (square footage, investment, capacity), blocked by a 403.
